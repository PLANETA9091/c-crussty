## 3. Effect size vs prediction — the inlining-barrier finding

Prediction (TASK-66/69/70): recoverable ≈ 0.7–1.2 CPU-s ≈ 1.6–2.7 % of burst.
Measured: **−5.0 CPU-s median (−8.1 %), −7.1 CPU-s mean (−10.0 %)** — 3–5× the
prediction. Mechanism (consistent with the JFR evidence below): the Java
`PerlinNoise.getValue` body (11 000 B, 8-iteration loop, megamorphic
`DoubleList.getDouble` dispatch + `wrap()` calls) was **not inlined** into the
worldgen caller loops; the patched body is a single `invokestatic`, which the
JIT inlines trivially — removing per-call dispatch overhead, keeping the
octave loop's register locality, and letting callers optimize across the call
boundary. The isolated microbenches (TASK-70/71: 0.815–0.825×) measured the
body in a dedicated loop and structurally could not see this cross-boundary
effect. Lesson recorded: for whole-method swaps, in-loop microbenchmarks give
a LOWER BOUND on real-context gains; the A/B remains the deciding evidence.
