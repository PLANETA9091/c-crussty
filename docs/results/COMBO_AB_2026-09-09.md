## 3. Mechanism interpretation (consistent with all measured evidence)

The G-AB win (TASK-74, −11.1%) came from swapping an 11,000-byte method
body whose megamorphic `DoubleList` loop created a JIT **inlining barrier**.
The improved-noise path is the opposite shape: `ImprovedNoise.noise(DDDDD)D`
is a SMALL body (sampleAndLerp + p) that inlines cleanly into its callers
(BlendedNoise octave loops, DensityFunction chains). For an inlined body the
whole-swap trades a zero-transition inlined call for a JNI transition
(35–90 ns floor, TASK-10 canon) — the measured three-arm probe (TASK-79
G-NORMAL rig) shows exactly this: one-crossing 615.9 ns vs two-crossing
655.6 ns vs pure-Java 1036.4 ns, but the production-realistic marginal is
only −39.7 ns/call, and the JFR `p(int)` samples were attribution of
INLINED code inside caller frames — removing the Java body also removes the
inlining, so the ledger nets out near zero. The combo A/B is the direct
confirmation: **whole-body swap ROI is inversely related to body
inlineability** — large bodies win (−11.1%), small bodies wash (~0%).
